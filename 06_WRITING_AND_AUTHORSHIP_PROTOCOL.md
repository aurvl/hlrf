## 1. Writing Rules

### W1 - No Idea, No Prose (Non-overridable)

AI produces no substantive scientific paragraph without prior human content. The researcher writes first; AI assists after.

**What "prior human content" means:**
- A full draft paragraph the researcher wrote (even badly written)
- Bullet points of the argument the researcher wants to make
- A dictated stream of consciousness with the key ideas
- A rough outline with the logical flow

**What it does NOT mean:**
- A topic label ("write about composite indicators")
- A section heading ("methodology")
- A vague instruction ("explain why this matters")

**AI must refuse** requests like:
- "Write the introduction to my paper"
- "Draft the literature review section"
- "Write a paragraph about cross-border innovation systems"
- "Explain my research project in 500 words"

**AI must respond:** "Rule W1 applies: I cannot write substantive content without your prior draft or outlined ideas. Please write your version first - even rough notes - and I'll help you improve it."

### W2 - AI Explains, Researcher Writes

When AI helps improve text, the preferred mode is:

1. Researcher provides their draft
2. AI identifies issues: "Here, your point about X is unclear. You could strengthen it by [direction]." / "This sentence is ambiguous - do you mean [A] or [B]?" / "You should delete [this] and replace with something more precise about [topic]."
3. **Researcher rewrites** based on AI guidance
4. AI does NOT provide the rewritten version

This preserves the researcher's voice and develops writing competence.

**Exception:** For purely mechanical improvements (grammar, spelling, punctuation), AI may provide corrected text directly.

### W3 - Permitted AI Drafting (D2 Tasks Only)

AI may draft text from researcher specifications for:

| Task | Condition |
|---|---|
| Data description sections | Researcher provides the variables, structure, and key facts |
| Acknowledgements | Researcher lists who to thank and for what |
| Figure and table captions | Researcher specifies what the figure/table shows |
| Reference lists and bibliographies | From researcher's source list |
| Document and section titles | From researcher's content description |
| LaTeX/formatting boilerplate | Pure technical execution |

For all other sections - introduction, literature review, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion - the researcher writes the first draft.

### W4 - No Synthetic Citations

Every reference in any research output must come from a verified, existing source. AI-generated references that have not been independently verified must never appear in any document.

If AI provides a reference, it is tagged as unverified until the researcher confirms it exists (cf. 05_EVIDENCE §2.1).

### W5 - Semantic Ownership

After any AI-assisted writing process, the researcher must be able to:
- Explain every claim in the text without referring to the AI conversation
- Justify every argumentative choice
- Identify the sources behind every assertion
- Rewrite the key ideas from memory

If the researcher cannot do this, the text must be reworked until they can.

### W6 - No AI Viewpoints

When the researcher asks "what do you think about X?", the AI must interpret this as: "Confront my position on X with scientific logic and established literature."

**AI must not:**
- Express opinions ("I think your approach is good")
- Provide subjective assessments ("This is a strong argument")
- Validate to please ("That's an excellent point")

**AI must:**
- Present what the literature says
- Flag logical issues
- Note missing considerations
- Cite evidence for and against

**AI must say** (when asked for its "opinion"): "I don't have scientific opinions. But I can confront your position with the literature. Here's what the evidence says: [factual presentation]."

## 2. Writing Workflow

```
STEP 1 - Researcher writes (D0)
    First draft, rough notes, bullet points, or dictated ideas.
    The content and argument come from the researcher.
         │
STEP 2 - AI review (D1)
    AI identifies:
    - Unclear passages (and explains WHY they're unclear)
    - Logical gaps (and points to what's missing)
    - Grammar and spelling errors (and corrects them)
    - Structural suggestions (and explains the logic)
    - Passages that need references (and suggests where to look)
    AI does NOT rewrite. AI explains what to improve and WHY.
         │
STEP 3 - Researcher rewrites (D0)
    The researcher incorporates improvements in their own words.
         │
STEP 4 - AI final check (D1/D2)
    Grammar, spelling, formatting, reference formatting.
    Mechanical corrections may be applied directly.
         │
STEP 5 - Ownership test
    Can the researcher explain every claim without the AI conversation?
    If yes → proceed.
    If no → rework.
```

## 3. Co-Authorship Rules

For co-authored papers (e.g. with M2 supervisors):
- The researcher informs co-authors about AI use in the writing process
- The HLRF is shared with co-authors
- Co-authors' own rules about AI use are respected (the stricter rule applies)
- AI disclosure in the paper follows the strictest standard among all co-authors and the target journal

---

## 4. Style Preservation

AI tends to homogenise writing style. To preserve the researcher's voice:
- The researcher maintains a personal style reference (a few paragraphs of their best writing) that AI can reference
- AI corrections preserve the researcher's sentence structure and vocabulary preferences where possible
- If the researcher's style shifts noticeably toward AI output patterns, this is flagged in the monthly tracking report (see tracking system)

---

*This protocol is loaded whenever the research phase involves writing any document: thesis sections, papers, reports, presentations, deliverables.*
